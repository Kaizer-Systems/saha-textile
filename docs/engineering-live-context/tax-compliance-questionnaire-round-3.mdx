## T1. Charts of accounts by kind of business

**T1.1** A partnership firm's own chart adds: partners' capital accounts (fixed) and current accounts, partners' drawings in the current accounts, loans from partners under unsecured loans, and a "Profit and loss appropriation" group holding interest on partners' capital and partners' remuneration. Is this the structure to ship?

- **(a) As drafted (Recommended).** For: fixed capital with current accounts is what most deeds use, and the appropriation group keeps interest and remuneration out of the firm's own expenses. Against: a deed with fluctuating capital needs the current-account entries posted to capital instead.
- **(b) Fluctuating capital.** One capital account per partner, no current accounts. For: simpler for small firms. Against: interest on capital is harder to work out on a moving balance.
- **(c) Something else.** Please describe it.

**T1.2** An LLP's own chart adds the same partners' items as T1.1 plus a "Reserves and surplus" group; the owners' group is "Partners' contribution". Is that right?

- **(a) As drafted (Recommended).** For: follows ICAI's guidance note on LLP financial statements (contribution, current account, reserves). Against: none we know of.
- **(b) Contribution only.** No current accounts. For: fewer ledgers. Against: the guidance note shows current accounts separately.
- **(c) Something else.** Please describe it.

**T1.3** A private limited company's and a one person company's chart adds: securities premium and general reserve (under reserves and surplus), dividend payable, provision for income tax, a "Deferred tax liabilities (net)" group, tax expense, directors' sitting fees, loans from directors and a CSR expense ledger. Anything to add or remove?

- **(a) As drafted (Recommended).** For: covers the Schedule III Division I headings a small company uses. Against: deferred tax assets and long-term provisions are not included.
- **(b) Add items.** Please list them (for example deferred tax assets, long-term provisions, capital reserve). For: a fuller chart on day one. Against: unused ledgers clutter a small company's books.
- **(c) Something else.** Please describe it.

**T1.4** A proprietorship's chart adds "Interest on capital" as an optional expense ledger and "Loans from the proprietor's family" under unsecured loans. Should interest on capital be there at all?

- **(a) Keep it, optional, noted as not deductible (Recommended).** For: some proprietors want it to see the return on capital. Against: it can be mistaken for a deductible expense.
- **(b) Leave it out.** For: no risk of a wrong deduction. Against: those who want it must add it themselves.
- **(c) Post it as a transfer to capital (appropriation).** For: shows it below the profit line. Against: a proprietorship has no appropriation account in the non-corporate format.

**T2.1** From 1 April 2026 every entry carries "393" without the table item. What should each show?

- **(a) The exact section 393 table item for each payment (Recommended).** Please write it against each row. For: the payment voucher and the return show the right reference. Against: none.
- **(b) "393" alone.** For: nothing more to maintain. Against: a reader can't tell the payments apart by section.

**T2.2** Are the rates and limits in the table right for FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27?

- **(a) Yes, all rows (Recommended if so).** For: the set goes live as it is. Against: none.
- **(b) Corrections.** Please write the corrected rate or limit against each row.

**T2.3** A proprietor deducts TDS under these sections only when the previous year's turnover passed the tax audit limit. Should the software apply that condition?

- **(a) Yes, from last year's turnover and the tax audit limit in RS-LIMITS (Recommended).** For: no deduction where none is due. Against: the turnover must be final before the year starts.
- **(b) Always deduct.** For: simple. Against: deducts where the law doesn't ask, and the supplier objects.
- **(c) A switch you set each year.** For: you decide. Against: it can be forgotten.

## T4. Statements (report templates)

Each statement adds up groups or ledgers of the chart. Income and expense ledgers count the year's movement; every other ledger its closing balance. The non-corporate balance sheet shows the year's profit under the owners' funds until the books are closed.

**T4.1** The non-corporate profit and loss account shows: revenue from operations (sales and direct incomes), other income, cost of goods sold and direct expenses, other expenses, and profit for the year. The non-corporate balance sheet shows: owners' funds (and, for a firm, partners' current accounts), profit for the year, borrowings, trade payables, other current liabilities, provisions, then property, plant and equipment, investments, inventories, trade receivables, cash and bank, loans and advances and other current assets. Is this enough for Level I to IV?

- **(a) As drafted, with the level's extra notes added later (Recommended).** For: one layout serves every level; the notes differ. Against: Level I needs more notes on day one.
- **(b) Separate layouts per level.** Please say what each needs. For: exact to the guidance note. Against: four layouts to maintain.

**T4.2** The level is worked out from turnover and borrowings each year. Which limits decide Level I, II, III and IV?

- **(a) The guidance note's limits, which you write here (Recommended).** We will add them to RS-LIMITS. For: the level follows the law. Against: none.
- **(b) You choose the level each year.** For: no limits to maintain. Against: it can be forgotten.

**T4.3** The Schedule III statement of profit and loss lists "Other expenses" by ledger (gateway and bank charges, rent, stock loss, cash differences, rounding, legal fees, security, advertising, directors' pay and sitting fees, CSR). An expense ledger the company adds later shows only in the profit line until it is added to a line. Is that acceptable?

- **(a) Yes; adding a ledger to a line is part of creating it (Recommended).** For: every line says exactly what it holds. Against: a forgotten ledger is missing from "Other expenses".
- **(b) "Other expenses" takes every indirect expense not shown elsewhere.** For: nothing is ever missed. Against: needs a rule the builder doesn't have yet.

**T4.4** A cash flow statement is not drafted yet. When is it needed?

- **(a) For the private limited company only, built with the year-end close (Recommended).** For: a one person company and a small company may leave it out (s.2(40)). Against: a private limited company that is not small needs it at its first year end.
- **(b) For every kind.** For: useful to the owner. Against: not required for non-corporate entities.
