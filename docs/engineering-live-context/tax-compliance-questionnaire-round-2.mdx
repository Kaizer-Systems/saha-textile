PART 0 — WHAT WE HAVE RECORDED AS SETTLED
================================================================================

We have taken the following from your replies and are building to it. Please
read this list and correct anything we have misread; otherwise no action is
needed on Part 0.

S1.  Place of supply cannot be derived from the shipping PIN code alone. Our
     system must capture and store, for every order: billing/recipient address
     with State and PIN, shipping/delivery address with State and PIN, the
     customer's GSTIN where applicable, and the determined Place of Supply as
     a stored field on the invoice.

S2.  For an unregistered (B2C) customer, place of supply is determined under
     Section 10(1)(ca) from the recipient address recorded on the invoice.

S3.  For a registered (B2B) customer where goods are moved, place of supply is
     where the movement terminates for delivery.

S4.  Supplies from West Bengal to a Union Territory are inter-State and attract
     IGST. We must not charge CGST + UTGST. CGST + UTGST is for an intra-UT
     supply, which does not arise for us.

S5.  Invoice-level HSN requirements and GSTR-1 reporting requirements are two
     different things and must be configured separately. Up to Rs 5 crore
     preceding-year turnover, 4-digit HSN is mandatory on B2B invoices; above
     Rs 5 crore, 6-digit.

S6.  Tax-inclusive display pricing is acceptable. The website may show
     "Saree Rs 5,250 — GST included". The tax invoice must separately show
     taxable value, CGST rate and amount, SGST rate and amount (or IGST rate
     and amount), and total invoice value.

S7.  A delivery charge for delivering the goods sold forms part of the value of
     supply under Section 15(2) as an incidental expense, and transport,
     packing and insurance can form a composite supply with the goods as the
     principal supply.

S8.  Exports may be zero-rated, and we will use Route A: export under LUT
     without payment of IGST, then claim refund of eligible accumulated ITC. We
     will obtain an IEC before regular export of goods and arrange AD-code /
     bank-account registration for the customs and export setup. Export
     invoices will carry the endorsement "SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT UNDER BOND OR
     LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX."

S9.  Gift cards: issuing/selling the card is not itself a taxable supply, no
     GST at the point of sale of the card, and GST applies normally on the
     underlying goods when the card is redeemed (CBIC Circular 243/37/2024-GST
     dated 31 December 2024). Redemption is a tender/payment mechanism, not a
     discount: if the taxable value of the goods is Rs 1,000, it stays
     Rs 1,000 even where the customer pays entirely with a gift card.

S10. Advance payments: operationally the document sequence is order
     confirmation / payment receipt, then dispatch, then tax invoice.

S11. Invoice numbering per Rule 46: consecutive serial number, unique for the
     financial year, maximum 16 characters, one or multiple series permitted,
     alphanumeric and special characters including hyphen and slash permitted.
     Format approved in principle: ST/26-27/000001, then ST/26-27/000002. A
     cancelled invoice number remains part of the sequence and is not reused.

S12. Credit notes: outer deadline is 30 November following the end of the
     financial year to which the supply relates, or the date of furnishing the
     relevant annual return, whichever is earlier. One credit note per original
     invoice/order — we will not combine multiple invoices into one credit note.

S13. Purchase master: for every supplier invoice we will capture supplier legal
     name, GSTIN, invoice number, invoice date, supplier address, HSN/SAC,
     product/service description, quantity, taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST,
     cess if any, place of supply, RCM flag, e-invoice IRN where applicable, QR
     code / e-invoice data where applicable, payment date, goods receipt date
     and purchase return details.

S14. Rounding: GST computed to two decimal places internally, final invoice
     payable amount rounded to the nearest rupee, with the adjustment shown on
     the invoice as a "Round-off" line (for example +Rs 0.40 / -Rs 0.40).
