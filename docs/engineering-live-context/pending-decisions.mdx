## How this file works

1. Every unresolved product, business, compliance, architecture, or operations choice lives here exactly once.
2. Roadmaps and progress files refer to the stable decision ID, not copied question text.
3. When the owner answers a decision:
    - record the final rule in `owner-decisions-log.mdx`;
    - reconcile affected architecture, contracts, code, and portal documentation;
    - remove the resolved decision from this file;
    - mark the corresponding roadmap/progress gate resolved.
4. Implementation tasks, credentials, external approvals, and ordinary TODOs do not belong here.
5. `project-context/nextjs-context/` is superseded and is never a decision source.
6. A decision may be **partially** settled. Where a `### Settled by CA confirmation` block appears
   inside an entry, those points are locked in `owner-decisions-log.mdx`, are not open, and must
   not be re-asked or re-decided; only the remainder of that entry is still a question. The
   outstanding question set issued on 2026-08-21 is `tax-compliance-questionnaire-round-2.mdx`.

## 01. `DEC-NUMBERING` — Business document numbering

### Decision needed

Choose the human-readable format and allocation policy for customer orders, tax invoices, purchase invoices, and future credit/return notes.

### Why this matters

Numbers appear in customer communication, PDFs, admin search, GST records, accounting exports, refunds, and audits. Tax-invoice numbers must remain unique, consecutive where legally required, and at most 16 characters.

### Recommended route

Use separate Indian-financial-year sequences for each document family. Allocate numbers transactionally and never reuse a cancelled number.

### Options

#### Option A — FY-reset sequences (recommended)

- Example: `O/26-27/0001`, `ST/26-27/0001`, `P/26-27/0001`.
- Pros: accountant-friendly, clean document-family separation, natural 1-Apr rollover.
- Cons: needs transactional rollover and length validation.

#### Option B — Perpetual sequences

- Example: `ST/00000042`.
- Pros: simplest allocation and global search.
- Cons: weaker financial-year readability and reporting ergonomics.

#### Option C — Custom

- Supply the exact prefix, separator, padding, reset rule, and gap policy for every document family.

### Settled by CA confirmation (2026-08-21) — no longer open

Locked in `owner-decisions-log.mdx` under **Tax, invoicing, and statutory compliance**; do not
re-ask these:

- Rule 46 constraints are binding: consecutive, unique within the Indian financial year, at most 16 characters, alphanumeric with hyphen and slash allowed, one or multiple series allowed.
- Separate FY-reset sequences per document family are confirmed acceptable, so Option A's shape is safe to build.
- Allocation is transactional, rollover is 1 April, and a cancelled number is never reused and stays in its sequence.

**Still open here:** the exact prefix, separator, padding and length for each document family.
The CA's own example breaks the limit — `STCN/26-27/000001` is 17 characters — so the formats
must be approved before any live document is generated. Build the allocator now, keep every
format string in configuration. Round-2 items `B6.1`–`B6.6`.

## 02. `DEC-TAX-HSN` — Tax classes, GST slabs, and HSN

### Recommended route

Category defaults with product/variant overrides. Snapshot resolved HSN, tax rate, taxable base, and tax amount on each order line. Obtain accountant confirmation before final tax contracts are frozen.

### Options

#### Option A — Category defaults plus product/variant overrides (recommended)

- Pros: efficient data entry with exception handling.
- Cons: requires clear precedence and publish validation.

#### Option B — Product/variant assignment only

- Pros: explicit and independent of multi-placement categories.
- Cons: repetitive and easier to omit.

#### Option C — One global tax rule

- Pros: simplest.
- Cons: brittle if fabric, stitched goods, or services differ.

### Settled by CA confirmation (2026-08-21) — no longer open

Locked in `owner-decisions-log.mdx` under **Tax, invoicing, and statutory compliance**; do not
re-ask these:

- Invoice HSN requirements and GSTR-1 HSN reporting are separate settings, configured separately.
- HSN digit length is turnover-banded: 4-digit up to ₹5 crore preceding-year turnover, 6-digit above.
- Resolved HSN, rate, taxable base and tax amount are snapshotted on the order line.

**Still open here:** the actual HSN codes and GST rates per category, whether the per-piece value
threshold for apparel applies and on which value it is tested, and whether the category-default
plus product/variant-override model is acceptable to the CA as a control. Round-2 items `B2.1`–`B2.8`.

**Architectural warning for anyone building before this closes:** if the rate turns on per-piece

### Recommended route

### Options

#### Option A — Ledger plus future credit seam (recommended)

- Pros: simple accounting and future-ready.
- Cons: no automatic credit redemption.

#### Option B — Active store credit

- Pros: useful for refunds/exchanges.
- Cons: balance correctness and checkout liability.

#### Option C — Wallet, top-up, and points

- Pros: maximum retention feature set.
- Cons: excessive launch and accounting scope.

### Settled by CA confirmation (2026-08-21) — partial

**Still open, and blocking:** the same question for loyalty points has no answer at all. Until the
CA rules whether redeeming points is a **discount** that reduces taxable value or a **tender**
that does not, no points redemption can be wired to checkout or to the invoice — the two answers
produce different tax on the same order. Round-2 item `A1`. The existing defer posture (Points
tiles visible but inert) is correct and should hold.

## 22. `DEC-DOMAIN-TOPOLOGY` — Production hostname topology

### Decision needed

Choose the permanent production topology for the storefront, admin, and API.

### Why this matters

The choice controls cookie domain/scope, credentialed CORS, CSRF origins, Nginx
routing, Cloudflare records, CSP/connect-src, OAuth callbacks, public runtime
config, and deployment/runbook paths.

### Recommended route

Use explicit subdomains: `www.sahatextile.com`,
`admin.sahatextile.com`, and `api.sahatextile.com`, with narrowly scoped
cookies and exact CORS/CSRF allowlists.

### Options

#### Option A — Separate subdomains (recommended)

- Pros: clear application/deployment boundaries and explicit API origin.
- Pros: straightforward Cloudflare/Nginx routing and operational ownership.
- Cons: requires careful cookie scope, credentialed CORS, and OAuth callback
  configuration.

#### Option B — Same-origin reverse-proxy paths

- Example: storefront at `/`, admin at `/admin`, API at `/api`.
- Pros: simpler browser-origin and cookie behavior.
- Cons: more coupled routing/deployment and a more complex proxy namespace.

#### Option C — Hybrid

- Example: storefront/admin share the apex while API uses a subdomain.
- Pros: may simplify one application boundary.
- Cons: combines both models and needs a precise exception matrix.

### Settled 2026-11-02 — no longer open

- Separate per-application subdomains (Option A shape) for staging and production.
- Single-level hostnames only, because Cloudflare's free Universal SSL covers just `*.sahatextile.com`.
- Staging: `staging.sahatextile.com` (storefront), `admin-staging.sahatextile.com`, `api-staging.sahatextile.com`, `cdn-staging.sahatextile.com`.

### Settled 2026-11-03 — no longer open

- Production storefront: the bare `sahatextile.com`, which the live WordPress site already uses as
  its main address; `www.sahatextile.com` permanently redirects to it. Keeping the host Google
  already indexes avoids a host change on top of the URL migration.
- Production admin, API and CDN: `admin.sahatextile.com`, `api.sahatextile.com`,

Still open: the cookie-domain rule — host-only cookies per application, or a shared

## 24. `DEC-PORTAL-PRIVATE-ACCESS` — Developer-portal private-access design

### Decision needed

Choose the identity allowlist, session policy, and origin-protection mechanism
for the entire developer-portal host.

### Why this matters

The static portal delivers canonical Engineering Live Context, search payloads,
Storybook, TypeDoc, Scalar/OpenAPI, and database-catalogue assets to the browser.
Protecting only HTML routes or hiding navigation would still expose the
underlying static assets. The public repository also means deployment controls
cannot turn committed secrets into private information.

### Locked baseline

- Access is default-deny and covers the whole host and every asset.
- There is no unrestricted “Everyone” rule or unrestricted email-OTP policy.
- `noindex` is defense-in-depth only.
- Unauthorized shell, static-asset, and direct-origin requests are release tests.
- Machine access uses scoped service tokens.

### Recommended route

Use a Cloudflare Tunnel so the portal origin is not publicly reachable, plus a
Cloudflare Access identity allowlist and short-lived sessions. Use scoped service
tokens for CI or machine readers.

### Options

#### Option A — Cloudflare Tunnel + Access (recommended)

- Pros: removes direct public origin exposure and centralizes identity policy.
- Cons: requires tunnel lifecycle and service-token operations.

#### Option B — Public origin restricted by authenticated-origin/JWT validation

- Pros: works with a conventional origin topology.
- Cons: more origin hardening and bypass testing; mistakes can expose static assets.

### Status note (2026-08-13)

Still **unanswered / pending**. This gate blocks **developer-portal deployment**
and production private-access work only. It does **not** block admin, storefront,
API, CRM, auth, MSG91 adapter, or local `portal:persistent` development.

**Local update 2026-08-20:** the loopback-only Identity Loom username/password
gate is implemented for `portal:persistent`, with process-memory sessions and no
